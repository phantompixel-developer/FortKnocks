# Art Direction Foundation

## Target
**Stylised gritty 2D post-collapse illustration**: readable and characterful rather than photorealistic, grimdark, or generic zombie imagery.

## Visual principles
- Strong silhouettes at phone size.
- Exaggerated but believable vehicle shapes.
- Clear separation between crew, cover, interactive props, and background.
- Damage states must be readable immediately.
- Dust, sparks, smoke, debris, recoil, and impact animation carry much of the perceived polish.
- Colour/value hierarchy should keep gameplay targets more readable than scenery.

## Layering
Typical battlefield presentation:
1. sky/atmosphere,
2. distant ruined environment,
3. midground structures/parallax,
4. gameplay terrain,
5. combatants/platforms,
6. interactive foreground props,
7. VFX and UI.

## 2.5D rule
2.5D may be used for parallax, turret rotation, layered vehicle parts, lighting tricks, or presentational depth. Core collision, trajectory, damage, and gameplay remain 2D unless a concrete design problem proves otherwise.

## Vehicle evolution
The visual story should move from:
- rust, mismatched panels, civilian wrecks, improvised barriers,
through
- reinforced pickups, welded protection, scavenged machinery,
to
- restored armoured vehicles, disciplined fortifications, and carefully maintained old-world hardware.

Later gear should look more capable without losing the salvage-built identity of the player's faction.

## Fort Knocks
The hub itself is a progression visualization:
- early: tarp, wrecked car, campfire, corrugated metal;
- mid: workshop, generators, welded gates, organised storage, armed vehicles;
- late: reinforced perimeter, communications, heavy garage capability, restored military hardware.

## Originality
Do not use another game's artwork as an image-to-image source for production assets. References may describe broad genre qualities, but final characters, silhouettes, UI, environments, props, and composition must have an independent design language.
