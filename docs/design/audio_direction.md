# Fort Knocks — Audio Direction

## Goal

Audio should make the small portrait battlefield feel physical, improvised and dangerous without becoming a noisy military simulation.

The first production-presentation pass establishes the cue language before committing to a large external audio library.

## Sound identity

Three broad families:

- **Salvage / mechanical:** short metal, clack, scrape and impact character for UI, gates, cover and platforms.
- **Ballistic / physical:** distinct launch weight, ricochet and material impact for projectile roles.
- **Recovered technology:** colder electrical tones for Shock Capsule, power cells and precision/old-world equipment.

Silence and restraint matter. A mobile player must be able to understand the shot and impact from a few important cues rather than a wall of constant effects.

## Current representative implementation

game/presentation/audio_director.gd provides a lightweight procedural audio proof:
- low looping ambience for hub/menu/battle contexts,
- standard fire cue,
- heavier low fire cue for Heavy Slug,
- brighter electronic launch for Shock Capsule,
- ricochet cue,
- crew hit,
- metal/cover hit,
- electrical surge,
- victory,
- defeat,
- UI confirm/back cues.

The implementation generates short AudioStreamWAV data at runtime. It is not intended as the final mastered soundtrack; it proves timing, cue ownership and sonic contrast without importing throwaway files.

## Music direction

Fort Knocks should avoid continuous heroic orchestral scoring.

Working direction:
- sparse low mechanical drones,
- restrained percussive pulses,
- scavenged-radio/industrial texture,
- tension rising around projectile commitment/impact rather than constant melody,
- stronger musical identity reserved for campaign regions, hub growth and major checkpoints.

Music must leave frequency/attention space for shot, ricochet and destruction feedback.

## Mixing priorities

Highest priority:
1. shot release,
2. impact / destruction / surge,
3. critical UI feedback,
4. ambient bed,
5. decorative texture.

On phone speakers, cues must still separate by timing, pitch and envelope even when low bass is lost.

## Production pipeline

Before batching final audio:
1. keep event names and ownership stable,
2. validate that each tactical event has a useful cue,
3. verify repeated shots do not become fatiguing,
4. replace procedural proof cues one family at a time,
5. normalise loudness consistently,
6. prefer mono/short compressed SFX where stereo adds no value,
7. record licence/source/provenance for every imported asset,
8. preserve a no-manual-setup Play flow.

## Provenance

The Milestone 3 audio pass is algorithmically generated by repository code and introduces no external samples, music tracks or third-party sound libraries.

Any future imported audio must have explicit source/licence provenance before shipping.
